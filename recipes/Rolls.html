Description

Wonderful, wonderful, wonderful--did I mention these are wonderful?? These cinnamon rolls are so unbelievably good!! They are perfectly sweet, fluffy, soft, spiced, and full of apple cider flavor! The rolls are a bit of a project to make, but I feel they're totally worth it. Other than leaving the walnuts out of the filling, I followed the recipe pretty closely. I did knead the dough (just because I love kneading dough!) for about 6-8 minutes; let it rest on the counter for 20 minutes, then placed it in a greased bowl and covered it. Then placed it right into the fridge to rise overnight. It worked great! I also found that my filling curdled when I added the apple butter to it. That made it a little difficult to spread on the dough; but I made it work. And when reducing the cider, it took me a lot longer--like 50 minutes. After removing the 1/4 cup for the icing, I kept cooking it down. And when it finally started getting 'syrupy', there was still about 2/3 cup of it. So I said heck with it, and added the cream to the 2/3 cup reduced cider. I poured the full amount over the rolls before baking and it worked just fine! My rolls did need an extra 5 minutes bake time, but that's no big deal. The rolls are delicious on every level, and they make your house smell AMAZING as they are baking! Thank you, Chef John, for the recipe!

Ingredients

    Dough

  • 1 cup plus 2 tablespoons warm milk (90 degrees F/32 degrees C)
  • 2 1/4 teaspoons active dry yeast
  • 1/3 cup white sugar
  • 6 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
  • 1 large egg, beaten

Steps

  1. Pour warm milk into a small bowl and sprinkle yeast on top. Allow to sit for 10 minutes. Add sugar, melted butter, egg, and salt. Whisk to combine. Add flour and stir with a wooden spoon handle until everything is well combined and a sticky dough forms. Cover and let sit for 20 minutes.
  2. Wet your hands. Uncover dough and do a set of 6 or 7 “stretch and folds” of the dough with your wet hands, turning the bowl as you go. Wet your hands again and shape the dough into a smooth ball. Cover and let raise until doubled in size, about 1 1/2 hours.
  3. Transfer dough to a work surface and press out the air. Grab dough from the edge and stretch it over the top to the center. Repeat about 5 times, going all the way around, until the dough comes together. Flip dough over and use your hand and a bench scraper to form the dough into a tight ball using a circular motion on the table. Set dough into an oiled resealable bag and refrigerate overnight.
  4. The next day transfer cold dough to a floured surface and press flat into a rectangle shape. Dust with flour and roll into a large oval. Dust again and flip dough over. Use you hands to gently stretch the dough to form a rectangle. Continue rolling until you have a rectangle that is approximately 18x12 inches.
